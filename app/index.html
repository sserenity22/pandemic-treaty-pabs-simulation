Countries adopted the WHO Pandemic Agreement in May 2025, but its most important part is still unfinished: the rules for sharing dangerous viruses and the vaccines, tests and treatments made from them (the PABS Annex). Rich and poor countries have barely moved on their red lines. This tool lets negotiators test whole deals and see who would get doses, who would pay and who would sign, before the World Health Assembly decides in May 2027.

What the app is for

GOAL 1

Help the Parties reach an annex

A working tool for IGWG-9 (2–13 November 2026), the sessions after it, and adoption at WHA80 in May 2027.

GOAL 2

Test whether positions achieve their aims

See what a package produces in an outbreak: doses to WHO, money to the system, and how many manufacturers sign PABS contracts.

GOAL 3

Stay neutral

One engine for every bloc. Every assumption is visible, sourced and adjustable, and coordinators set their own bloc’s priorities.

GOAL 4

Show every bloc the cost of its hardline

Maximal terms push manufacturers out of an opt-in system, so fewer doses arrive. Pure voluntarism leaves an empty system. The same test runs for everyone.

GOAL 5

Put a price on “willingly undertaken”

Cost to manufacturers of each benefit-sharing option in Ebola-type, H1N1-type, COVID-type and novel-pathogen scenarios, so commitments “on mutually agreed terms” can be weighed in money and doses.

GOAL 6

Negotiate packages, not clauses

Submit positions on all seven key issues of the term sheet at once, compare packages, and find trades that bring every Party over the line.

Your concerns, answered

Delegations test positions here that they would never say in the room. The app is built so that nobody, including the people who built it, can see what you test.

Do I have to say who I am?

No. There is no account, no login and no country field. The app never asks which delegation you represent.

Does anyone see the packages I test?

No. Every calculation runs in your browser. Nothing you choose or type is sent to a server, logged or tracked, and the page has no analytics.

Can a package be traced back to my bloc?

Packages carry no country name. A share code holds only the positions and your notes, and it travels only through channels you choose.

What about the closed-session text?

It stays with your delegation. Today the app uses only the public 30 April 2026 text. In the prototype, your delegation loads its own copy into its own private workspace; it is never pooled or shared.

Does testing a package commit us to anything?

No. A package tested here is not a position, a proposal or a signal. It has no standing in the negotiation.

Who decides what my bloc cares about?

You do. Change your bloc’s weights and corrections on the Blocs tab; the defaults are a first coding from public statements, with sources.

Is it tilted toward one side?

The same engine and the same test run for every bloc, and the hardline test shows each side what its maximal package costs. Every assumption is open to challenge in Method.

What does stay on my device, and can I remove it?

Saved packages and your weights stay in this browser only.

One limit to know: the page itself sends nothing, but the service hosting it can log that it was opened (for example, your IP address, or your account if you are signed in to that service). For sensitive sessions, open it from a device and network your delegation is comfortable with.

How a bloc coordinator uses it

  1. Build your package: a position on each key issue, with your own wording in the notes.
  2. Read the verdict: signable or not, which Party blocks it, and what it delivers in each scenario.
  3. Set your weights so the model reflects what your bloc cares about most.
  4. Compare your draft with another bloc’s text and see which issue moves the result.
  5. Find trades that lift the least satisfied Party without dropping doses below your floor.
  6. Share the package code inside your bloc, or copy a brief for capital.

What to know before you start

  • Base text: the public on-screen annex text of 30 April 2026 (A79/8). Loading your delegation’s current text privately is the next prototype step.
  • Private and anonymous: no login, nothing sent to a server. See your concerns, answered.
  • Illustrative: the model is not validated. Bloc positions are a first coding from public statements, for coordinators to correct.
  • Not a negotiator: it proposes trades for discussion. It does not predict votes or speak for any bloc.

How the model works

      
Two signatures matter. Parties sign the annex; manufacturers sign WHO PABS Contracts. A package must clear both.

      
Parties. Each issue position carries a coded reaction per bloc (−2 to +2, the average of the clauses it sets). A bloc's acceptance is the weighted average, scaled 0–100. A coded red line is a veto. The package is signable when all four state blocs reach the threshold with no veto.

      
Manufacturers. Benefit-sharing binds only those who sign (Art. 12.6(a); annex II.C.1). Leverage (what staying out costs them) and burden (share of emergency-window revenue given up, plus a compliance load) set participation:

      
participation = 0.95 × (0.3 + 0.7 × leverage) × 1 / (1 + e^((burden − tolerance)/0.06))
doses to WHO = participation × production × (donation % + affordable %) × delivery + licensed regional supply
lives (benchmark) = doses × lives per 1,000 timely doses × e^(−delay/45 days)
